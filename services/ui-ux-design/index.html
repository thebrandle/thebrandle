What you get

Flows before pixels

  • Research and flows

    Who uses it, what they’re trying to do, and every step between opening the app and getting it done.

  • Wireframes and prototypes

    Clickable drafts you can put in front of real people before anyone writes code. Cheap to change, which is the point.

  • Interface design

    Type, colour, spacing and states for every screen, including the empty, loading and error ones people forget.

  • A design system

    Buttons, inputs, cards and rules, built once as components. Screen two hundred then matches screen one.

  • Web and mobile

    Layouts for phones, tablets and desktops that respect how each one is held and used.

  • A usable handoff

    Organised Figma files, specs, exported assets, and us on a call when a question comes up.

The honest bit

Do you need this?

If people keep asking how to do the main thing your product does, yes. Design fixes that for less than the support tickets cost. It also stops developers guessing, and guessing is slow.

Good fit if you are

  • Designing a new product or a first version
  • Sitting on an app that works but confuses people
  • A developer who’d like someone else to make it look right
  • Growing fast, with screens that no longer match

How it goes

Insight to interface

  1. 1

    Understand

    A free call, then a look at your users, your competitors and whatever exists already. A fixed quote follows.

  2. 2

    Structure

    User flows and wireframes. Ugly on purpose, so the conversation stays on what it does.

  3. 3

    Design

    The interface, screen by screen, with a prototype you can click through.

  4. 4

    Hand off

    Components, specs and assets in Figma, plus a walkthrough with whoever is building it.
